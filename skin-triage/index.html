Can skin photos help people decide when to seek care?

A larger model scored better at classifying skin images. None of the three extra pretraining methods we tried improved its score.

By The Anh Nguyen · Updated 5 October 2026 · Local research draft

Offline review draft Category-prediction research. Not medical advice or a validated care-guidance product.

The product goal is to help people decide when to seek care. This study tests whether a larger classifier improves predictions across eight skin-image categories.

A larger model Extra pretraining What it means

1. Does a larger model help?

An encoder turns an image into numbers describing its patterns. A classification layer uses those numbers to predict a category. We tested whether a larger encoder made those predictions better.

C50 became the research benchmark. It passed the agreed improvement checks, but still made substantial errors in some categories.

Can a larger encoder separate the categories better?

We first asked whether a larger classifier would improve category predictions. C18 used a ResNet18 encoder. C50 used a ResNet50 encoder with a wider final feature vector. We expected C50 to separate the eight categories better.

An encoder turns image pixels into numerical features. A classification layer combines them into category scores. Together they form the classifier. Both encoders are convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which learn image patterns from nearby pixels.

C18 and C50 differed in encoder architecture and classification-layer width. Each started from its own pinned ImageNet weights and trained on the same skin-image data. The results show whether C50 improved the benchmark, but they do not isolate network depth as the cause.

Give both alternatives the same comparison

Each family trained three members for 17 epochs apiece, using the same seed list and training recipe. An epoch is one pass through the training images. A seed controls repeatable random choices, including the new classification layer's starting weights, image order and augmentation.

Each family's ensemble prediction averages the category probabilities from all three members.

The scoring images had already been used in development. They can support a development decision, not an independent estimate of smartphone performance.

C50 passed the declared comparison

The main score was macro-F1, which balances missed cases and incorrect predictions while giving all eight categories equal weight. On the 0–100 scale below, higher is better. It is not classification accuracy.

All three criteria passed, so C50 replaced C18 as the research benchmark. Every category's F1 point score increased. That does not mean every error measure improved: SCC recall, the fraction of its labelled cases found, fell from 37.89 to 36.84 on a 0 to 100 scale.

Two weak categories make the limit concrete. C50's F1 was 33.96 for dermatofibroma and 38.46 for SCC, with only 36 and 95 scoring images respectively. Passing relative improvement criteria did not establish absolute adequacy or consumer readiness.

C50 gave us a stronger benchmark, not a finished product. The next comparison kept the ResNet50 architecture and tested a different idea: could another image-learning task give the encoder a better starting point for category training?

2. Can extra learning help C50 further?

Would practising a different image task first teach useful patterns? We tested three alternatives without category labels. Every encoder already had general-image pretraining. New runs did not reuse trained C50 or start from scratch.

The new training setup differed from original C50, so we also compared against labels-only training for 17 and 25 passes.

All 36 training runs and final evaluation finished. None of the three recipes passed the preset replacement checks. This does not show that pretraining can never help.

Can pretraining improve on C50?

Three earlier learning tasks. One final classification goal.

Can an extra image-learning task help a classifier distinguish eight skin categories? We tested rebuilding an image, recovering it after corruption, and connecting two views of the same image. None qualified to replace C50. All 36 training fits and final quality evaluation completed.

We gave each encoder a new starting point for category learning. Category predictions determined whether that helped.

1. Follow one image to its category scores

The same two-part classifier from the C18/C50 comparison is our final task. Here the encoder is always ResNet50, producing 2,048 feature values. A fresh classification layer turns those features into eight category scores.

An earlier task could give the encoder features that help distinguish the categories. That did not guarantee better predictions. We tested whether these tasks improved the final classifier.

2. Give the encoder a different task first

All new runs started from the same pinned ImageNet weights. None used C50's trained weights. The experiment tested further pretraining of an already-pretrained encoder.

Each candidate spent eight epochs on its earlier task. An epoch is one pass through the training images. All used the same 14,639-image training portion. Those images already had category labels; this stage did not use them.

3. Carry the weights, not the pictures

At the end of pretraining, we keep each candidate's encoder weights and attach a fresh classification layer. The classifier then learns from the original images and labels. We measure whether pretraining improves category predictions.

The encoder kept training during Stage 2. It computed fresh features on each forward pass as its weights changed. DAE's added corruption stopped after pretraining. The training run does not establish that the encoder learned optimal features.

Each recipe trains three independent members, using seeds 3019, 3029 and 3039. Their category probabilities are averaged into one ensemble result per recipe. Separate frozen-encoder probes were diagnostic only; their classification layers did not supply weights to these classifiers.

4. Compare against ordinary training, not just another task

A gap from C50 alone would not tell us whether the earlier task helped. C50 tests replacement of the historical benchmark. SUP17 checks the new protocol without pretraining. SUP25 checks spending the extra eight epochs on labelled training instead.

SUP25 matches source-image epoch exposure, not computing cost, label use or optimizer history. The reconstruction models and VICReg do different work per image.

The new runs kept BatchNorm running statistics fixed, while historical C50 trained them. These statistics normalize internal values; they are not the encoder's full weights. The encoder's trainable weights still changed. SUP17 helps account for the protocol difference, so we cannot attribute a gap from C50 to pretraining alone.

Retain C50 as the benchmark. No tested recipe qualified to replace it.

The criteria were fixed before training. Replacement required passing every control comparison, uncertainty checks, category-level limits and probability-quality limits.

The training run completed, but none of these recipes met the replacement criteria. The result applies to these recipes. It does not identify why they fell short or show that pretraining cannot help in general.

This is not evidence of clinical readiness. The scoring data were reused in development, and C50 still struggles with some categories. Smartphone and clinical performance remain unestablished.

Completed training and verified final evaluation

We completed 36 fits across 411 epochs, with 94,119 successful optimizer updates. The final evaluation completed 42 prediction passes. The parent and child processes exited with status 0. All 20 C50 parity records passed, so the replay matched the saved baseline within the fixed tolerance. We rehashed the final arrays and report. The training ledger did not change.

We kept the earlier evaluation attempts in the record. We do not know how much work the original final attempt completed or how it exited. The first recovery completed one pass, then stopped at baseline parity. A corrected evaluation adapter completed the comparison without retraining or changing thresholds. We observed preprocessing skew, but it does not prove that skew caused the earlier failure.

C50's class F1 remains 0.3396 for dermatofibroma and 0.3846 for SCC. The selection set contained 36 and 95 images for those categories. These small samples limit precision. Keeping C50 as the benchmark does not establish clinical adequacy.

The encoder experiment had its own predeclared criteria, separate from the earlier C18/C50 architecture gates. What this means

Keep C50 as the research benchmark. The larger architecture helped; these extra learning tasks did not meet the bar.

That is a category-prediction finding, not a care recommendation. Performance on ordinary smartphone photos remains unknown, and the complete care-guidance workflow remains unvalidated.

Project scope, remaining questions and supporting evidence

What would it take to help someone decide whether to seek care from a skin photo? Predicting a category is only one part of that question. A model can improve on a benchmark without establishing that it can guide a person.

This project addresses the product goal separately from the completed experiments. We compared C18 and C50, then tested three earlier image-learning tasks against C50. Both comparisons used development data. Neither tested performance on ordinary smartphone photos using an independent set.

A category prediction is not yet care guidance

An eventual product would take a smartphone photo of one localized skin concern and supporting context. Before offering guidance, it would need to check whether the image is suitable and within the supported scope. It would also need to relate category evidence to an appropriate next step.

The eight-category model cannot by itself establish whether an arbitrary photo is supported, whether care is needed, or how urgently. Those are different questions. This report tests category prediction, not those product outcomes.

Where this leaves the project

The architecture comparison made C50 the stronger category benchmark. None of the three pretraining methods met the replacement criteria. C50 remains a research benchmark, not a care-guidance system.
